# Model and asset pipeline

High-quality hero assets are authored and inspected in Blender, exported as GLB and consumed by Three.js. Blender MCP is an interactive control and viewport-feedback bridge; it does not replace modelling judgment, topology, UV work, materials, rigging or visual review. Python-based automation is retained for reproducible scene setup and export.

Required source provenance for every shipped asset:

- Editable source `.blend`, generation/export script where applicable, and exact output GLB.
- Model author/source, license or explicit ownership, and permitted redistribution.
- Front, side, rear and close-up evidence, plus the final in-game camera view.
- Animation names, coordinate conversion, scale, triangle/material counts and texture dimensions.

The runtime expects actual GLB models for the robot players, ball, table, paddle and two environments. It must surface a useful loading error when models cannot be loaded; a missing final model must not silently become an unrelated primitive approximation.

The previous billiards project provides the robot design language (ivory/cyan and graphite/amber), selected material ideas and animation references. Its programmatic billiards pose system is not used as a table-tennis action rig. New motions require ready, serve, forehand, backhand, sidestep, recover and result poses; wrists and paddle grip require close-up checks, and the swing's contact pose must match the actual ball-contact tick.

Before public release, populate the shipped asset manifest with actual file hashes, source paths and render evidence. Passing TypeScript and physics tests does not validate the model quality or claim that a Blender refinement step has happened. The original `pong.html` contains no reusable 3D model assets.

## Rebuilding and coordinate contract

Create `assets/sources/` and `assets/models/` before executing scripts. Run them in a dedicated Blender scene: they clear the active scene. The MCP bridge validates the scripts before execution and returns viewport feedback after execution; modelling changes are reviewed and iterated in that actual Blender scene.

- For the robot script, prefix `ASSET_ROOT` with the absolute path to this repository's `assets` directory.
- For the equipment/arena script, also prefix `BUILD_ASSET` with `table-tennis-equipment`, `cyber-arena` or `sports-hall`.
- Scripts save the source `.blend` and matching GLB; `.blend1` and other numbered Blender backups are excluded from Git. They are local recovery files, not a second authoritative source.
- Run `npm run assets` after rebuilding. Its content-hashed publish filenames and manifest are generated from the current canonical GLBs. `predev` and `prebuild` run this preparation automatically.

Authoring uses metres, Blender Z-up, forward −Y. GLB export uses standard glTF Y-up; the browser maps the core's `(x,y,z)` to Three's `(x,z,-y)`. Do not add a second arbitrary scale or up-axis rotation to individual equipment meshes.

`Table`, `Net`, `Ball` and `Paddle` are separate named roots in the equipment GLB. The table top is 0.76 m above the floor, with 1.525 × 2.74 m playing surface. The net is 0.1525 m high. The ball's mesh origin is its centre, radius 0.02 m. The paddle root is the grip origin; `PaddleContact` identifies the rubber contact centre for wrist alignment. The exported placement of the ball and paddle is only an inspection arrangement; gameplay supplies their transforms.

Robot clips exported in the current file: `Idle`, `Ready`, `ServeHold`, `ServeToss`, `Serve`, `Forehand`, `Backhand`, `Recover`, `StepLeft`, `StepRight`. Browser code blends clips and applies wrist/contact alignment; a clip existing is not proof of a successful live return. Rigid panels are weighted to articulated bones and batched by 15 body regions, with the right elbow bearing and pivot separate for first-person visibility: 17 mesh objects and 53 material primitives replace the initial 164 mesh objects. Finger and thumb weights remain articulated inside each hand batch. Runtime draw-call cost must still be measured; two robot instances are not assumed to be free.

`GripR` contains the complete paddle attachment pose. Attach `Paddle` with identity translation/rotation, without another half-turn. Paddle blade-up is glTF +Y, its front face +Z; `PaddleContact` is approximately `(0, 0.139, 0.0055)` in that frame. `PalmL` and `BallHoldL` identify the bare left palm surface, with node local +Y as its outward normal in glTF. A held ball centre is one radius above this surface. Released balls follow the core simulation, never a parented hand. Stroke contact is authored at normalized time 0.5; the toss clip's release pose is 0.32. The browser maps these poses to authoritative serve/contact ticks rather than waiting for arbitrary clip completion.

The ready and stroke clips lower the pelvis by 8 cm, solve both leg segments toward fixed ankle positions, and keep the foot orientation planted. The stroke adds only 6 mm of weight movement; sidesteps lift alternating feet by 2.6 cm. This is a knee bend, not a downward translation of the whole model through the floor. [Five actual Blender motion frames](visual/robot-athletic-frames.png) show the crouched stance and level soles through the stroke, without visible detached leg parts. These inspection frames do not include the game's final paddle IK or ball.

## Evidence and current limits

The included images are actual Blender viewport captures. They show a complete robot silhouette, equipment supports and net, a cyber arena with seating and suspended ring structure, and a sports hall with trusses, benches and warm floor panels. Early arena builds had overlapping floor surfaces; the foundations were lowered while preserving the playing floor at Z=0, and the linked images are the resulting inspection views.

These still images do not validate real-phone frame rate, touch comfort, live racket-ball synchronization or multiplayer latency. Browser interaction and performance evidence should be recorded separately, together with the exact model hashes loaded. No native-source edit is considered shipped until the prepared manifest and host build contain its new GLB hash.

The older [right-hand four-view close-up](visual/robot-hand-angles.png) is historical evidence from 2026-10-05 and does not depict the current hand. On 2026-10-07 the hand was rebuilt around the real paddle: index support on one rubber face, thumb on the opposite lower face, and the remaining three fingers around the handle. [The current front/back grip and left-palm views](visual/2026-10-07/README.md) include actual equipment and were inspected through Blender MCP. They show the intended mechanical shakehand silhouette without an obvious free handle or fingers passing through the blade. They do not establish photorealistic anatomy or contact forces. New authored stroke key poses also show bent elbows, torso rotation and planted knees; runtime paddle alignment and camera comfort remain separate checks.

The equipment update keeps geometry, scale and contact origins unchanged. Its white ABS ball now uses subtle 256px colour variation and roughness 0.32 instead of coarse mottling. The tabletop has a deeper blue, roughness 0.86 and reduced dielectric specular response to retain white-ball contrast under bright lighting.

The two venue selection images are actual Cycles renders from the canonical arena and equipment GLBs, composed with camera and lights by [`capture-venue-preview.py`](../art/capture-venue-preview.py). They are not generated concept art. Prefix `ASSET_ROOT`, `THEME`, `OUTPUT_FILE` and `SOURCE_FILE`; keep the composed inspection `.blend` outside canonical model sources. The 2026-10-07 host images are `/images/table-tennis/cyber-arena.png` and `/images/table-tennis/sports-hall.png`. Their framing and lighting serve the selection card; gameplay uses its own camera and lighting.
