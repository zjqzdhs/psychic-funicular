# Robot audit · 2026-10-07

## Findings against the released 261007.16 asset/runtime

This records the previous release before the corrective changes below. The release screenshot is `docs/visual/2026-10-07/game-held.png`; it visibly places both near arms in a crossed cluster and cannot demonstrate natural joint motion.

- **P1 — Floating shoulder roots.** `src/browser/scene.ts` formerly moved each near shoulder to `hand target + (±0.15, -0.23, 0.38)` independently. A read-only reproduction loading the actual GLB and sampling default ServeHold at 0.1 s moved the right shoulder from 1.402 m to 0.797 m, and the left from 1.295 m to 0.834 m. Shoulder-to-body attachment was intentionally broken to put arms inside the camera frame.
- **P1 — Contact alignment overrides joint plausibility.** The former two-bone solver constrained segment length, but not shoulder/elbow planes, forearm twist, wrist bend or inter-arm clearance. Low contact height also forced the blade sideways. The same reproduction added approximately 111° / 70° of local wrist rotation relative to the authored right/left hand poses. These are pose differences, not clinical joint-angle measurements. Right/left wrist world X ended at -0.208 / -0.240 m, crowding both hands into one side.
- **P2 — Torso turn uses the wrong local axis.** `art/build-cyber-player.py` wrote torso turn to local Z. The actual exported rest skeleton has local Y along the torso's vertical axis; local Z points forward. The supposed turn therefore rolls the torso sideways. The archived backswing and follow-through images visibly show this lean.
- **P2 — The previous rig check cannot accept anatomy.** `scripts/check-rig.mjs` recorded only paddle-centre and palm-centre distance. Its sampled face normal was fixed, its test view had no camera/GPU, and it checked neither shoulder attachment nor elbow/wrist motion or arm overlap. A small contact error is not a natural-animation pass.

## Reference evidence

The repository records Table Tennis England articles about shakehand grip, ready position, forehand phases and service/spin. It does not contain a documented review of another table-tennis game's controls or a frame-by-frame human video motion study. The two close-up grip images show equipment attachment; they do not validate the full shoulder–elbow–wrist chain in the runtime camera.

## Correction record

The runtime now keeps both shoulders attached to the body. It solves the elbow toward a feasible wrist direction and uses the paddle's free roll around its physical face normal to avoid folding the wrist; it does not rotate the collision face or move the collision contact to make the image look aligned. Elbow bearings remain visible.

An intermediate correction was rejected in the actual browser: the old camera was still 2.55 m behind the table while the robot stood in front of it, exposing large shoulder roots. The corrected view comes from the exported head's visor point, with wider peripheral view and a downward table gaze. The complete athlete uses its physical player position; no first-person shoulder relocation remains. [Current held-ball gameplay view](../visual/2026-10-07-correction/game-held.png) shows the full table and connected forearms entering from the lower edges. The near right forearm remains visually large because it is close to the eye; this is a stylized mechanical model, not photorealistic anatomy.

The torso's turn axis and the serving palm direction were corrected in the Blender authoring source. The canonical `.blend` and GLB were rebuilt through the installed Blender MCP, followed by actual MCP camera inspections of [forehand](../visual/2026-10-07-correction/blender-forehand.png), [serve hold](../visual/2026-10-07-correction/blender-serve.png) and [backhand](../visual/2026-10-07-correction/blender-backhand.png). Local MCP receipts are retained on D: under `D:/DevTools/BreakBuilderNative/table-tennis-20261007/robot-audit-correction-build.json` and `audit-*-look.json`. These images inspect authored poses; the gameplay image separately inspects the runtime camera and inverse kinematics.

The targeted rig check now also asserts shoulder attachment, finite elbow/wrist angles, tilted face-normal alignment and forward-facing serving fingers. With the corrected standing position, its 192 sampled poses reported: shoulder drift 0 mm, rounded contact error 0 mm, elbow angles 69.2–149.6°, maximum wrist bend 63.5°, face-normal error below 0.001°, and serving wrist bend 3.9°. These are rig-coordinate angles for this mechanical skeleton, not biomechanical measurements. TypeScript and scoped ESLint passed. This is a small regression check and static visual correction, not proof that every future stroke, camera movement or real-phone session is natural.

The current round also reviewed Table Tennis England and PingSkills teaching pages, and Table Tennis Touch's official control description. A Vimeo navigation timed out and a YouTube embed was unavailable, so no claim is made that a full human video was watched or motion was traced frame by frame.
